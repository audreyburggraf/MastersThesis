In Figure \ref{fig: StokesI_POLI_grid}, we present the cleaned \SI and debiased polarized intensity images of IRS 63 at \bfourNS, \bfiveNS, \bsixNS, and \bsevenNS, overlaid with the polarization vectors that make up the observed polarization morphology. As the observing wavelength decreases, there is a clear transition in polarization morphology. \SC{Mention that the polarization vectors are normalized to focus on showing angle only.}


At \bfourNS, the polarization vectors are mostly azimuthal, forming a circular pattern around the centre of the disk. However, near the centre of the disk, there is a small region where the vectors are aligned with the minor axis of the disk. As the wavelength decreases from \bfour to \bsevenNS, the centre region where vectors are aligned with the minor axis becomes larger, while the azimuthal pattern resides only on the outer part of the disk. 

The patterns at \bfive and \bsix are more of a transition, but at \bseven the uniform alignment consistent with dust self-scattering is clearly dominating. \note{fix this sentence}
