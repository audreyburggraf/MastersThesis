Spherical dust grain modelling is a much more common approach in the field due to easier calculations and shorter commutation time. However, as mentioned previously, it is though that it is more realistic that the dust grains in disks are non-spherical. The change in shape affects the scattering properties of the dust grains, which is where the difficult part of the modelling comes in. Non-spherical (irregular) dust grain modelling has become more accessible with \texttt{glitterin} from \cite{glitterin}, a neural network for dust grain light-scattering. An example of six irregular grain shapes are shown in Figure \ref{fig: glitterin_dust_grains}.
