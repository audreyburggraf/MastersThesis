
IRS 63 is a Class I protostar embedded in the L1709 region of the Ophiuchus molecular cloud at a distance of approximately 144 pc \citep{sadavoy_dust_2019, Dom2020}. It is one of the brightest Class I protostars at (sub)millimetre wavelengths, and is surrounded by a relatively large circumstellar disk with a radius greater than 50 au \citep{Dom2020}. 


The brightness and proximity of IRS 63 make it an ideal target for polarization observations. Additionally, its disk allows the polarization morphology to be studied across the inner and outer disk. Lastly, since IRS 63 is a Class I protostar, it also provides the opportunity to investigate dust grain growth during the early stages of planet formation \citep{Dom2020}. 




Previous studies of IRS 63 at \bsix dust continuum with 5 au resolution, presented by \cite{Dom2020}, revealed substructure in the disk (Figure \ref{fig: IRS63Dom2024}). Polarization observations at the same wavelength, with 35 au resolution, presented by \cite{sadavoy_dust_2019}, revealed the polarization morphology. This morphology shows an azimuthal pattern in the outer region of the disk, with uniform alignment at the disk centre. These observations show that IRS 63 has disk substructure and detectable polarization emission. However, current polarization observations are only available at a single wavelength. Multi-wavelength polarization observations are needed to determine the dominant polarization mechanism and place constraints on the dust grain size.
