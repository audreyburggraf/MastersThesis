The interstellar medium (ISM) is the material within a galaxy between the stars \citep{mckee_dynamical_1999}. The cold, dense regions of the ISM, mostly composed of molecular hydrogen, are known as molecular clouds \citep{Kuruwita}, and their structure is shown in Figure \ref{fig: molecular_cloud}. Within molecular clouds, dense regions can form, called pre-stellar cores \citep{Kuruwita}. When a pre-stellar core becomes gravitationally unstable, it collapses under its own gravity, marking the beginning of the star formation process \citep{Kuruwita}. Once the disk is gas-less and has negligible mass, the Class III stage has been met, and the central star is a pre-main
sequence object \citep{Kuruwita}. 

Throughout the evolutionary stage of the object from Class 0 to Class III, the object transitions from a young star embedded in an envelope to being a main-sequence star with a disk more analogous to the Solar System. An illustration of this process is shown in Figure \ref{fig: star-formation-schematic}.
