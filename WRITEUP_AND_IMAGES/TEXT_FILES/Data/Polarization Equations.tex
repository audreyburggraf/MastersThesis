\subsection{Polarization Fraction and Angle}


\SC{Here is where you can mention that you took the equations from Coude et al. (2019).}


Using the debiased polarized intensity, the (debiased) polarization fraction can be calculated by:
\begin{equation}
    \POLFeq = \POLIeq/I,
    \label{eq: POLF}
\end{equation}
\noindent where $I$ is \SI intensity. The uncertainty on polarization fraction is:

\begin{equation}
    \POLFerrEQ = \POLFeq \left[\left(\frac{\POLIerrEQ}{\POLIeq}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\SIerrEQ}{I}\right)^2\right]^{1/2}.
     \label{eq: POLF_error}
\end{equation}
   


\SC{It could be worth mentioning that these are half-vectors.  That is, you have a 180 degree ambiguity with polarization angles (e.g., you can't tell 0 deg from 180 deg).  True vectors have magnitude and direction, but with polarization vectors, we don't have full direction. Somewhere it would be good to say that these are plane-of-sky polarization vectors.}


\SC{Specify that these angles are measured in radians.  Also note that zero degree is defined as up with angle increasing counter clockwise (north to east).}
The polarization position angle, $\theta$ is calculated by: 
\begin{equation}
\theta = \half \tan ^{-1} \frac{U}{Q},
    \label{eq: PA}
\end{equation}
\noindent with an uncertainty calculated by:

\begin{equation}
    \PAerrEQ = \frac{1}{2} 
    \frac{\sqrt{(U\SQerrEQ)^2 + (Q\SUerrEQ)^2}}{Q^2 + U^2}.
    \label{eq: PA_error}
\end{equation}
