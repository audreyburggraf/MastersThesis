\section{The Basics of Interferometry}
% ------------------------------------------------

The most basic interferometer consists of two telescopes configured to observe the same astronomical object \citep{Monnier}. When the two radio waves arrive at the same location, they can either have constructive interference, where the result is a bigger wave, or destructive interference, where the result is a smaller wave, or even cancel each other out \citep{Monnier}. The radio wave from the object typically arrives at one telescope before the other \citep{Monnier}. \SC{It may help to have a cartoon show the two element interferometer showing the path delay} The signals collected at each telescope are brought together to a central location and combined to create an interference pattern, known as a fringe \citep{Monnier}. Longer baselines make finer fringes, providing higher angular resolution and are more sensitive to smaller structure \citep {Perley2026}. Alternatively, shorter baselines make larger fringes with lower angular resolution and are better for large-scale targets \citep {Perley2026}.
