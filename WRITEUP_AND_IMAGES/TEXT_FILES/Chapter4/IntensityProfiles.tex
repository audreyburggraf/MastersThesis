The slices in \SI and polarized intensity for each wavelength are presented in Figure \ref{fig: slices_grid}. The synthesized beam for each wavelength is overplotted for a comparison. The beam is plotted as a one-dimensional Gaussian with a standard deviation calculated from the beam full width at half maximum (FWHM) along the minor axis using the equation:
\begin{equation}
    \sigma = \frac{\text{FWHM}}{2\sqrt{2 \ln{2}}}.
    \label{eq: FWHM_to_sigma}
\end{equation}
\noindent The Gaussian was normalized to 1m and scaled by the peak intensity of each slice. 
% \noindent The standard deviations are used to calculate the Gaussian shape at each offset using:
% \begin{equation}
%     \text{Beam(offset)} = \exp{\left(-\frac{\text{offset}^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)},
% \end{equation}
% \noindent which was then normalized to 1 and scaled by the maximum value of the slice. 




The \SI profiles (top row of Figure \ref{fig: slices_grid}) show a single peak centred on the centre of the disk at all four wavelengths. This is as expected since the protostar at the centre of the disk should be giving off the most emission. However, in the debiased polarized intensity profiles (bottom row of Figure \ref{fig: slices_grid}), we do not see the same trend. The profiles are consistently offset to the right of the centre position at all four wavelengths. The offset is larger than the synthesized mean, meaning that it is spatially resolved.


A similar signature of polarized intensity being brighter on the near-side of the disk was shown in \cite{Yang}. This indicates that the disk is optically thick and flared, meaning that the disk scale height increases with radius (Figure \ref{fig: dust-grain-growth-schematic}). The angular resolution of the data is not high enough to explore this signature and resolve smaller structure in the disk.
