\subsubsection{kabs, sca vs lambda, \cite{Kataoka_2015} Figure 1}
% ----------------------------------------------
To begin the spherical dust modeling, one of the first steps was to ensure that I could recreate figures that have been make previously. In Figure \ref{fig: Kataoka2015Figure1Recreation}, I present my recreation of Figure 1 from \citep{Kataoka_2015}. In this figure we show the absorption and scattering opacities in units of \cmsq per gram as a function of wavelength for a maximum graim sizes of 1 \micron and 100 \micron. 

We can see that at a smaller dust grain (red lines, \amax = 1 \micronNS), the absoprtion opacity is much greater than the scattering opacity. 

For the larger dust grain (blue lines, \amax = 100 \micronNS), from 0.1-1 mm ($10^2 - 10^3$\micronNS), the scattering efficiency is larger than absorption.

\subsubsection{-Z12/Z11 vs polarization degree, \cite{Kataoka_2015} Figure 2}
% ----------------------------------------------
In Figure \ref{fig: Kataoka2015Figure2Recreation}, I replicated Figure 2 from \citep{Kataoka_2015} for the four bands I am looking at. In these plots we look at how polarization changes with scattering angle. Here, we plot \quoted{the degree of polarization due to single scatting averaged by the size distribution for several maximum grain sizes} \citep{Kataoka_2015}. $N_{11}$ is calculated in Equation \ref{eq: N11}. 

We see that when we are looking at smaller grains with a size of 10 \micron and 100 \micron the polarization degree peaks at a scatting angle of $\theta$ = 90\deggNS.
