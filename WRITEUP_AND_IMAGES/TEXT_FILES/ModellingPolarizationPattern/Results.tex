\subsubsection{Gaussian Model}
The five best-fitting Gaussian models (out of 150 tested) are presented in Table \ref{tab:gaussian_top5}. The best fit at each wavelength is highlighted in Figure \ref{fig: GaussianBestGrid}, with the observed vectors in black and the model vectors in red. Additionally, the Gaussian ($W_{\text{Uniform}}$, Equation \ref{eq: gaussian_eq}) for each best-fit are shown in Figure \ref{fig: gaussian_best_map_w_uniform}.  


From the results in Table \ref{tab:gaussian_top5}, BMAJ and BMIN remain the same among the top five models at each wavelength, indicating that these values provide a good fit among the tested values. The parameter that varies is the $\Phi$ parameter, which controls how flat the Gaussian is \SC{It isn't just phi.  Please be clearer here.}. A higher $\Phi$ value produces a flatter Gaussian and a faster transition from uniform to azimuthal polarization (see Figure \ref{fig: Methods/GaussianExamples}). In the best-fit model at each wavelength, $\Phi$ is greater than 2, producing a flat-topped Gaussian rather than a standard Gaussian. This can also be seen in Figure \ref{fig: gaussian_best_map_w_uniform}, which shows that the area of uniform alignment increases as wavelength decreases. This indicates that uniform alignment, which is consistent with dust self-scattering, contributes more at shorter wavelengths, which is what we expect from Chapter \ref{sec: c4_polarization_maps} and the results from the ratio model.

\subsubsection{Ratio vs Gaussian Models}

The reduced $\chi^2$ values and visual comparisons of the ratio and Gaussian models show that the Gaussian model provides a better overall fit. \SC{order, see pdf}


Figure \ref{fig: RatioBestGrid} shows that, while the ratio model matches many of the observed vectors at each wavelength, it is a poor match for others. This is a limitation of applying the same polarization pattern across the whole disk. As discussed above, the polarization pattern changes with distance from the centre of the disk. Therefore, while the ratio model provides a good first test, it is an oversimplification to accurately model the pattern throughout the entire disk. 


Figure \ref{fig: GaussianBestGrid} shows that, although not all vectors match perfectly, many are accurately reproduced by the Gaussian model. At \bsix and \bsevenNS, the model does not match the vectors along the lobes, which could be related to depolarization. \question{go into more detail about this?} \SC{It may be good to remind the reader of the assumed 2\% polarization fraction, which could be underestimating the azimuthal pattern relative to the uniform pattern.} Overall, the Gaussian model is the better model but still has limitations, as it is only geometric.
