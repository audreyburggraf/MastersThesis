The results of the top 5 (out of 150) best (lowest $\chi^2$ value) Gaussian model comparison are presented in Table \ref{tab:gaussian_top5}. Additionally, the Gaussian ($W_{\text{Uniform}}$, Equation \ref{eq: gaussian_eq}) for each best-fit are shown in Figure \ref{fig: gaussian_best_map_w_uniform}.  


From the results in Table \ref{tab:gaussian_top5}, we observe that for the top 5 cases, the parameters of BMAJ and BMIN remain the same at each wavelength, indicating that these parameters were a good fit out of the tested values. The value that changes is the $\Phi$ parameter, changing how flat the Gaussian. In terms of the pattern, a higher $\Phi$ value produces a flatter Gaussian and a faster drop off from uniform to azimuthal (see Figure \ref{fig: Methods/GaussianExamples}). In all of the best-fit (top 1 at each wavelength) the value of $\Phi$ is much larger than 2, do the Gaussian is not rounded, and quite flat. This can also be seen in Figure \ref{fig: gaussian_best_map_w_uniform}. The changing of all three of these varibles tells us that at each wavlelength the area of uniform vectors changes (as BMAJ abd BMIN) changes, is always a flat shape and not a gradual typical Gaussian smooth drop off ($\Phi$ > 2). Looking at Figure \ref{fig: gaussian_best_map_w_uniform} again, it is clear that the area of uniform alignment in the fit increases as wavelength decreases, indiating that uniform alignment (dust self-scattering) dominates more at shorter wavelengths, which is what we expect from Chapter \ref{sec: c5_polarization_maps} and saw in the results from the ratio model.

\subsubsection{Ratio vs Gaussian Models}

The $\chi^2$ values and visual examples of both the ratio and Gaussian fits show that overall the Gaussian model is a much better fit. 


By inspecting the ratio model with Figure \ref{fig: RatioBestGrid}, we can see that while the model matches many of the vectors well at each wavelength, simultaneously it does a poor job at matching others. This is a limitation of the generalization of the same pattern across the whole disk. As discusses before, the dominant mechanism (pattern) changes as you move further from the centre of the disk. So, while the ratio model was a good start, ultimately it is an oversimplification to accurately model the pattern throughout the entire disk.
