Modelling the polarization from scattering for the multi-wavelength data allows us to place a constraint on the dust grain size using a method adapted from \cite{Zhang2023}, which is detailed later. To begin, two grain geometries were explored in these models: spherical grains and irregular grains. Both use the same dust composition and grain-size distribution (Chapter \ref{sec: model_assumptions}), but have different optical properties due to their shapes. Spherical dust grains are more commonly used since their scattering and absorption properties are much less computationally expensive. Alternatively, irregular dust grains are the more likely shape \citep{Lin2024}, but require more computation power. 


The geometric modelling of the observations in Chapter \ref{sec: methods/modelling polarization pattern} were used to identify the region of the disk at each wavelength where the observed polarization morphology is uniform along the minor axis, which is consistent with dust-self scattering. This region provides a constrains for the dust grain size (Chapter \add{reference}). The goal of the modelling is to model the dust properties in this region with spherical and irregular grains, and to compare the amount of polarized light to the observed polarization fraction from IRS 63 to constrain the dust grain size.
