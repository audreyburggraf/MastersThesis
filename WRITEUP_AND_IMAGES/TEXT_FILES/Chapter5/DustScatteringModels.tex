

Two grain geometries were explored in these models: spherical grains and irregular grains. Both use the same dust composition and grain-size distribution (Chapter \ref{sec: model_assumptions}), but have different optical properties due to their shapes. Spherical dust grains are more commonly used since their scattering and absorption properties are much less computationally expensive. Alternatively, irregular dust grains are the more likely shape \citep{Lin2024}, but require more computation power. 


The geometric modelling of the observations in Chapter \ref{sec: methods/modelling polarization pattern} were used to identify the region of the disk at each wavelength where the observed polarization morphology is uniform along the minor axis, which is consistent with dust-self scattering. This region provides a constrains for the dust grain size (Chapter \add{reference}). The goal of the modelling is to model the dust properties in this region with spherical and irregular grains, and to compare the amount of polarized light to the observed polarization fraction from IRS 63 to constrain the dust grain size.

\subsection{Measuring Observed Polarization Fraction}
\label{sec: measured_POLF}
% ----------------------------------------------
A single polarization fraction value was obtained at each wavelength to compare to the dust scattering models. The models should be compared to the observations by taking the value in with evidence of self-scattering (uniform minor axis alignment). Three methods were tested to determine the best value:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{F, max\text{ }I}}$: The polarization fraction at the pixel with maximum \SI.
    \item $\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{F, max\,\POLIeq}}$: The polarization fraction at the pixel with maximum polarized intensity.
    \item  $\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{F, Gaussian}}$: The mean polarization fraction within the uniform area from the Gaussian model ($W_{\text{uniform}}$= 1 $\pm$ 0.001, Equation \ref{eq: gaussian_eq}). 
\end{enumerate}
\noindent 

\noindent The values from these three methods at each wavelength are summarized in Table \ref{tab: polf_comparison}. The best representation of the observations was method 1, where the polarization fraction was taken at the pixel with the maximum \SI value. This selected value is referred to by the observed polarization fraction, and is denoted by \POLFobsNS.
