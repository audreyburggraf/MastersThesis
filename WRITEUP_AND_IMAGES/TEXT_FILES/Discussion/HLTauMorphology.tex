


A similar wavelength-dependent change in polarization morphology observed in IRS 63 is also observed in HL Tau. Figure \ref{fig: HLTauLin2024} shows that the polarization morphology for HL Tau is mainly azimuthal for longer wavelengths, and polarization vectors become increasingly aligned with the disk minor axis as wavelength decreases \citep{Stephens_2017, Li2024}. This similar trend in both HL Tau and IRS 63 suggests that this wavelength-dependent change in polarization morphology is not unique to either disk. 

A difference in the polarization morphology of HL Tau is that the two morphologies do not show a spatial dependence found, such as the one found in IRS 63. Specifically, the vectors at \bfour are almost all azimuthal, and the vectors at \bseven are almost all aligned with the disk minor axis, rather than showing signatures of contribution from both polarization mechanisms. In comparison, IRS 63 showed a distinct spatial combination of these morphologies, with the azimuthal pattern remaining on the outer disk and alignment with the disk minor axis in the centre. 

 
This similar trend in both HL Tau and IRS 63 suggests that this wavelength-dependent change in polarization morphology is not unique to either disk, and may be caused by the same changes in the contribution of the polarization mechanisms with wavelength. However, the difference in the spatial dependence suggests that there is another property causing differences in the contributions of the polarization mechanisms. A possible explanation could be that IRS 63 is a younger disk than HL Tau, and differences in evolutionary stage could contribute to these differences.
