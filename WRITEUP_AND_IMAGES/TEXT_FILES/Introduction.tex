\subsection{Disk Polarization Morphologies}
Magnetic Field Alignment:
\begin{itemize}
   \item\ \quoted{Classically, polarization is expected from non-spherical grains aligned with the magnetic field \cite{Stephens_2017}}.
   \item\ \quoted{Spinning dust grains in the interstellar medium are expected to align with their short axes perpendicular to the magnetic field of radiative torques  \cite{Stephens_2017}}.
\end{itemize}


Methods that are not magnetic field alignment:
\begin{itemize}
    \item\ \quoted{One polarization mechanism is caused by self-scattering from dust grains of sizes comparable with the wavelength \cite{Stephens_2017}}.
    \item\ \quoted{The other mechanism is due to grains aligned with their short axes along the direction of radiation anisotropy \theircite \cite{Stephens_2017}}.
    \item\ \quoted{Given this alignment, the polarization of thermal dust emission at millimeter and sub-millimeter wavelengths is expected to be perpendicular to the magnetic field \cite{Stephens_2017}}.
    \item\ \quoted{The polarization morphologies usually were not consistent with what would be expected from magnetically aligned dust grains \cite{Stephens_2017}}.
    \item \quoted{The mechanisms causing millimeter-wave polarization in protoplanetary disks are under debate\cite{Kataoka_2017}}
    \item \quoted{This indicates that dust grains are sligned with the major axis being in the azimuthal direction, which is consistent with the theory of radiative alignment of elongated dust grains, where the major axis of dust grains is perpendicular to the radiation flux} \cite{Kataoka_2017}
\end{itemize}


Explaining what the polarization looks like:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \quoted{We interpret the polarization at Xmm to be dominated by the grain alighment with the radiative flux producing azimuthal polarization vectors, while the self-scattering dominates at X mm and produces the polarization vectors parallel to the minor axis of the disk } \cite{Kataoka_2017}
\end{itemize}

Working for explaining what mechanisms each band looks like it has:
\begin{itemize}
    \item\ \quoted{The morphology at \bandsevenmm matches the expectation for self-scattering, while that at other wavelength shows a mix between self-scattering and grains aligned with the radiation anisotropy \cite{Stephens_2017}}.
\end{itemize}


Mechanism changes with wavelength:
\begin{itemize}
    \item\ \quoted{By showing two distinct polarization morphologies and a transition between the two, this paper provides definitive evidence that the dominant (sub)millimeter polarization mechanism transitions with wavelength \cite{Stephens_2017}}.
    \item \quoted{Furthermore, the morphology of the polarization vectors at X mm is completely different from those at X mm} \cite{Kataoka_2017}
\end{itemize}


Disentangling:
\begin{itemize}
   \item\ \quoted{Polarized emission at mid-infared wavelengths can occur due to absorption, emission and sometimes scattering, causing difficulty in interpreting the polarization morphology \cite{Stephens_2017}}.
   \item \quoted{To disentangle the polarization mechanisms, we observe the protoplanetary disk around IRS 63 with the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA)} \cite{Kataoka_2017}
\end{itemize}

Unsorted:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \quoted{Observations have shown the existence of a wide range of disk polarization morphologies \cite{Stephens_2019}}. 
    \item \quoted{At sub-millimeter wavelengths (around \bandsevenmm), disk polarization often occurs due to self-scattering \cite{Stephens_2019}}. 
    \item \quoted{Caption idea: Polarization pattern changing with wavelength doe the disk. The colour scales show polarized intensity. The polarization morphology is azimuthal at Band 3 and uniform at \bandsevenmm. At \bandsixmm, the morphology appears to be a mix of the two \cite{Stephens_2019}}. 
    \item\ \quoted{The mechanism for producing polarized emission from protostellar disks at (sub)millimeter wavelengths is currently uncertain\cite{Stephens_2017}}.
    \item \quoted{We successfully detect the polarized emission} \cite{Kataoka_2017}
\end{itemize}
